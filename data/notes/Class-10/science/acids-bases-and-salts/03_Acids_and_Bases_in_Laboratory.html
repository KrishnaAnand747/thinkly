Acids and Bases in Laboratory

In the laboratory, acids and bases are classified based on their strength and concentration. Neutralization reactions are important for preparing salts.

Strength of Acids and Bases

  • Strong Acids: Completely ionize, e.g., HCl, H2SO4
  • Weak Acids: Partially ionize, e.g., CH3COOH
  • Strong Bases: Completely ionize, e.g., NaOH, KOH
  • Weak Bases: Partially ionize, e.g., NH4OH

Concentration

  • Concentrated: High amount of acid/base in solution
  • Dilute: Low amount of acid/base in solution

Neutralization

Acid + Base → Salt + Water
e.g., HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

💡 Insights for Inquisitive Minds

Strong acids have low pKa values, indicating higher acidity. The degree of ionization determines strength, but concentration affects reaction rate. Neutralization is exothermic, releasing heat.

🎯 Scoring & Study Tips

Remember strong acids: "HCl, HBr, HI, HNO3, H2SO4, HClO4". Strong bases: Group 1 and 2 hydroxides. For neutralization, balance the equation: Acid H+ + Base OH- → H2O.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Don't confuse strength with concentration; a dilute strong acid has fewer H+ than concentrated weak acid. Avoid thinking all acids are strong; acetic acid is weak. Remember safety: handle with care, use PPE.
