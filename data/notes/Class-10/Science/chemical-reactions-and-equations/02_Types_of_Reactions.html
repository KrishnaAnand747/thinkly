Types of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions are classified based on how reactants combine or change.

Combination Reaction

Two or more substances combine to form one.
A + B → AB
Example: CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2

Decomposition Reaction

One substance breaks into two or more.
AB → A + B
Example: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2

Displacement Reaction

One element replaces another in compound.
A + BC → AC + B
Example: Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu

Double Displacement Reaction

Exchange of ions between compounds.
AB + CD → AD + CB
Example: NaCl + AgNO3 → NaNO3 + AgCl

💡 Insights for Inquisitive Minds

Reactions are driven by energy changes. Combination often exothermic, decomposition may need heat. Displacement follows reactivity series.

🎯 Scoring & Study Tips

Remember "Combi: Combine, Deco: Break, Disp: Replace, Double Disp: Exchange". Use examples: Photosynthesis (combi), Electrolysis (deco).

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Don't confuse displacement with double displacement. Avoid wrong examples; decomposition of water needs electricity. Remember precipitation in double disp.
