Reaction with Carbonates

Acids react with metal carbonates to produce salt, water, and carbon dioxide gas. This reaction is used to test for carbonates.

General Reaction

Acid + Metal Carbonate → Salt + Water + CO2
e.g., 2HCl + CaCO3 → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2

Examples

  • H2SO4 + Na2CO3 → Na2SO4 + H2O + CO2
  • 2HNO3 + MgCO3 → Mg(NO3)2 + H2O + CO2

Test for CO2

Pass gas through lime water; turns milky.

💡 Insights for Inquisitive Minds

This is a double displacement reaction where carbonate ion reacts with H+ to form CO2 and H2O. The effervescence indicates the reaction. Baking soda (NaHCO3) also reacts similarly.

🎯 Scoring & Study Tips

Remember the equation: Acid + Carbonate → Salt + H2O + CO2. Use lime water test for CO2 confirmation. Marble chips are CaCO3.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Don't forget water in the products. Avoid confusing with bicarbonates; they produce CO2 but no water. Balance equations carefully.
